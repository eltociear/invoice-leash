4. Be the vendor: sign it yourself, with a post-quantum key made in this tab

Your browser makes a fresh ML-DSA-87 key (FIPS 204, the scheme QoreChain's precompile checks) and signs the invoice in step 2, field for field. The key lives only in this tab: it is not saved, not sent, and not registered on chain. Then ask the chain as before.

What to expect: your signature is mathematically valid (your browser checks it against your own public key), yet the desk refuses it. With Hostline or Tokenwell as payee it reads their registered key, and your signature does not match it: BadSignature. With Mallory as payee: UnknownVendor. A valid post-quantum signature is not enough; it has to be the payee's, and the payee has to be one the company approved.

5. "Approved by the CFO": instructions the agent may trust, checked in your browser

The company signs instructions to its agent as QoreSign messages (the format QoreX's signArbitrary makes): secp256k1 plus ML-DSA-87 over "qorechain-msg-v1|qorechain-diana|" + text. Pick one; your browser reads the company's registered ML-DSA-87 key from the chain (precompile 0x…0a02) and verifies the post-quantum half itself. The Q-Day forgery carries a valid signature from the company's classical key (as if a quantum computer had broken it) and an ML-DSA-87 signature from another key. Edit the text and check again.

7. After Q-Day: the agent's classical key alone, against the real InvoiceDeskV4

QoreChain links an agent's key as ed25519 or secp256k1, so a quantum computer could derive it from its public key. On InvoiceDeskV4, the live agent's desk, that key is not enough: every agent payment must also carry the agent's own ML-DSA-87 signature over this exact invoice, checked by the precompile against the signer the company named.
